Discover Media Club

Life moves fast. Learn to capture the moments you will want to remember—from family adventures to the biggest moments at FIS—through photography, film, and live production.

See our work
FIS student media team at the Frankfurt Tech Show
What we do

Turn real moments into stories people remember.

Media Club brings students together behind the camera and inside the control room. We learn by doing: planning coverage, making creative decisions, and delivering work for real school events.

Produce live

Build livestreams and recordings as a coordinated crew—camera operators, directors, mixers, OBS operators, and supervisors working as one team.

Capture the moment

Learn how framing, light, timing, sound, and movement turn an ordinary photo or video into something worth keeping.

Shape the story

Direct, edit, mix sound, create graphics, and interview people. The finished story depends on thoughtful choices before, during, and after filming.

Inside Media Club

Every production has more happening behind the scenes.

You can start with one role, watch how the whole crew works, and gradually take on more responsibility as your confidence grows.

Live production

Think together. React together.

In the control room, students monitor camera feeds, choose shots, manage graphics and sound, and communicate with the crew. It is fast, collaborative, and one of the best ways to understand how live media works.

On camera

Learn to see before you press record.

Camera work is more than pointing at the action. You will practise composition, focus, movement, anticipation, and communication—skills that also make your everyday photos and videos better.

Good to know

Questions before you join?

Contact alex_westcott@fis.edu or ilia_katulin@fis.edu.

Do I need experience?

No. Beginners are welcome, and you can learn through guided practice before taking responsibility at an event.

What grade levels can join?

Media Club is open to FIS students in Grades 6–12.

How much time does it require?

It depends on the role and event. You can begin with training and choose opportunities that fit your schedule; major productions require more preparation and commitment.

Can I try different roles?

Yes. Exploring camera, control-room, editing, audio, and presenting roles is encouraged before you decide where to specialise.

Media Role Guide

Every great production is a team effort. Explore the responsibilities, expectations, and equipment behind each role before choosing your place on the crew.

Applies to every student role

Stream Expectations

A reliable stream depends on preparation, clear communication, safe equipment handling, and respect for everyone taking part in the event.

10 minready before start
Phase 01

Before live

Arrive, check in, and prepare every system.

  1. Arrive ready. All students, regardless of role, must arrive and be fully ready at least 10 minutes before the scheduled stream start. Equipment must be checked, headsets connected, and everyone at their assigned position.
  2. Report changes early. If you are running late or cannot attend, update your signup and inform a supervisor as early as possible so your role can be covered.
  3. Check in first. Report to the Adult Supervisor or Student Supervisor when you arrive. Do not change roles or positions without their approval.
  4. Complete technical checks. Cameras must be framed and focused, audio monitored, overlays and scores prepared, and the stream or recording connection confirmed before going live.
Phase 02

On air

Stay aware, communicate clearly, and protect the coverage.

  1. Keep comms professional. Headset communication should be short, calm, and relevant to the production. Avoid unnecessary conversation while live.
  2. Know when you are live. Camera operators do not have to verbally respond to every instruction, but they must acknowledge and remain aware when their camera is live or is about to go live. Hold the requested shot steadily and avoid unexpected changes.
  3. Stay focused. Avoid personal phones, unrelated conversations, and other distractions unless there is an emergency.
  4. Handle equipment safely. Keep food and drinks away, report faults or unsafe cables immediately, and never attempt an unfamiliar technical change without permission.
  5. Do not show injuries on stream. If someone is injured, camera operators must move away from close-ups and Mixing must avoid putting the injury on air. Use a neutral wide shot until it is appropriate to return.
Phase 03

After the stream

Close down correctly and leave the space ready.

  1. Finish the job. Wait until a supervisor confirms the stream and recording have ended correctly, then return equipment, tidy the production area, and report any problems.
Supervision & leadership
01
Adult only

Adult Supervisors

This role is for the adults present to support the production. They know what they are doing, so do not change or claim this role.

02
Experienced crew

Student Supervisor

Student leaders or experienced members who can run the stream with less adult help. An adult's explicit permission is required. Student Supervisors cannot also cover Photography because they must remain in the stream area.

Production control
03
Live direction

Mixing

The mixer chooses which camera feed goes live and calls “camera — prep/live” over the headsets. They relay a shot caller's direction, or make the calls themselves when there is no shot caller.

04
Stream systems

OBS

OBS controls the production computer. For sports streams this includes instant replays and the scoreboard overlay. The operator also monitors the stream or recording uplink and sound.

Camera crew
05
Remote camera

Robocam

Operates a remotely controlled camera using a monitor, joystick, and zoom rocker. Robocam carries the same responsibility as a handheld operator: follow the mixer's direction and film the action.

06
Core camera

Handheld

The backbone of many streams. Handheld operators film the action from outside positions, take direction through a headset, and send their pictures to the Regie. The usual cameras are Sony PXW-X70s.

07
Mobile camera

Wireless

Shares the handheld operator's responsibilities but can move freely while sending video wirelessly to the Regie. Usually uses a Panasonic Lumix GH6 on a gimbal and is reserved for experienced members.

08
Independent

Photographer

Photographers independently decide which parts of the action to capture. They may use a Media Club loan camera or the personal camera selected in their signup. Club camera reservations include a compatible lens and the complete kit must be returned after the event. Personal equipment is optional and is brought and used at the owner’s own risk, so keep it supervised and protected. Typical club cameras include the Canon 70/450/600/700D, with R6 and GH6 bodies reserved for experienced users or special occasions with adult approval.
